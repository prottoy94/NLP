# Milestone 1 test - AI text detection

| Paper | Goal/Problem Addressed | Methodology | Key Innovation | Main Findings/Results | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ['Your AI Text is not Mine': Redefining and Evaluating AI-generated Text Detection under Realistic Assumptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04906v2) | Misaligned and under-specified AI-generated text detection (AITD) notions, incompatible across datasets, not reflecting real-world human-AI co-creation. | Systematically defines AITD notions, creates AITDNA benchmark of human-machine co-constructed texts, and evaluates existing detectors. | A systematic framework for AITD notions and AITDNA, a new benchmark reflecting realistic human-AI co-creation. | Existing datasets are unrealistic; detector performance varies significantly by AITD notion; content/intent-based AITD are hardest to detect. | AITDNA collected in lab conditions; authorship-ID AITD not fully tested; current research relies on simplifying assumptions. |
| [PASA: A Principled Embedding-Space Watermarking Approach for LLM-Generated Text under Semantic-Invariant Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10977v2) | LLM watermarking vulnerability to semantic-invariant attacks (e.g., paraphrasing) and lack of robust, distortion-free generation. | PASA: principled, robust, distortion-free watermarking in latent embedding space using shared randomness and two-stage sampling. | PASA, an embedding-space watermarking method robust to semantic-invariant attacks while preserving text quality and offering principled error control. | PASA outperforms baselines against paraphrasing, achieves near-perfect detection, preserves text quality, and is computationally efficient. | May degrade under very strong rewriting; challenging for very short texts; requires tokenizer-compatible detector-side SLM. |

**Overall insight:** Paper 1 highlights the fundamental issues in defining and evaluating AI-generated text detection, showing that current methods and datasets are often misaligned with realistic human-AI co-creation. It calls for more robust, notion-aware detectors. Paper 2, on the other hand, offers a practical solution for watermarking LLM-generated text, specifically addressing robustness against semantic attacks. While Paper 1 identifies a critical gap in understanding "what to detect," Paper 2 provides a strong technical approach for "how to detect" if a watermark is present. The gap remains in developing detection methods that are robust across the diverse and realistic AITD notions identified in Paper 1.
